  • DAS

    Reliable cell signal in every corner of large buildings, from hospital wings to warehouses.

    A Distributed Antenna System (DAS) is essentially an indoor cellular network. Instead of relying on cell towers outside to push signal through concrete, steel and multiple floors, it distributes the carriers’ signal throughout the building. The goal is simple: reliable cellular connectivity for employees, customers and visitors, wherever they are inside. We help you evaluate the building’s coverage needs, coordinate with the carriers and find the most cost-effective approach, which isn’t always a full installation.

    See how DAS worksShow less

    Reliable cellular connectivity throughout your building

    Eliminate cellular dead zones, improve indoor coverage, and keep your employees, customers and visitors connected, from the first floor to the top floor and everywhere in between.

    How it works

    1. 1Carrier signal sourceThe system receives cellular signal from one or more carriers: AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile and others.
    2. 2Headend / equipment roomThose signals are combined, amplified and prepared for distribution through the building.
    3. 3Distribution networkFiber or coaxial cable carries the signal to antennas on every floor.
    4. 4Indoor antennasSmall, unobtrusive antennas in hallways, offices, meeting rooms, common areas and parking levels provide consistent coverage.
    5. 5Reliable coverageEmployees, visitors and emergency responders can make calls, send texts and use mobile data throughout the building, even where signal usually can’t reach.

    Key benefits

    • Improved indoor coverageStrong, consistent signal in basements, stairwells, elevators and interior rooms, not just by the windows.
    • Multi-carrier connectivityOne system can carry AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile, so everyone in the building is covered no matter whose plan they’re on.
    • More reliable communicationsStaff, customers and first responders stay reachable, which matters most in hospitals and other places where a dropped call is more than an inconvenience.
    • Built for large, complex buildingsPicture a hospital with ten floors, several wings, elevators and a basement. Without DAS, doctors lose reception in some rooms and visitors can’t reliably call out. The same goes for warehouses, campuses and parking structures.

    DAS is not Wi-Fi

    DASWi-Fi
    Improves cellular coverageProvides wireless network access
    Carries the mobile carriers’ serviceUses the building’s own internet
    Helps cellular calls and textingSupports applications and internet access
    Can support multiple carriersCarrier-independent
    Can support public safety radio with a separate systemDoes not replace public safety radio coverage

    They complement one another. Most large buildings end up needing both.

    Four questions before you invest

    A full multi-carrier DAS is a significant project, so we start by working out whether you need one at all.

    1. 1CoverageWhere are the dead zones, and how much square footage needs to be covered?
    2. 2CarriersDoes the system need to support AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile, or all three?
    3. 3InfrastructureDoes the building already have fiber, coax or pathways the system can use?
    4. 4FundingWill the carriers contribute to the cost, or will the building owner fund the installation?

    Sometimes a smaller cellular enhancement system, Wi-Fi calling, or a combination is the better answer. If first responders also need radio coverage inside the building, that’s a separate public safety system, and we can help you plan both.
